What the old calamansi tree remembers

One backyard tree holds a family history of summer drinks, shared harvests, and the patient work of tending something over time.

By Mara Villanueva July 18, 2026 6 min read

The tree was already taller than the roof when my grandmother moved into the house. No one remembered who planted it. Its branches leaned over the back wall, offering half their fruit to the neighbors and keeping the rest within reach of the kitchen window.

Every few weeks, someone arrived with a woven basket or an old enamel bowl. The smallest fruit went into dipping sauces. The fragrant ones were sliced into cold water. Anything left by Sunday became a jar of marmalade.

A measure of the season

There was no calendar for the harvest. We knew it was time when the branches lowered and the green skins began to hold a trace of yellow. After rain, the whole corner of the garden smelled bright and clean.

Some trees mark time more faithfully than clocks. They remember who watered them and who returned to gather fruit.

The work was ordinary: loosen the soil, trim a dry branch, carry water across the yard. Still, each task belonged to a longer rhythm. The tree answered care slowly, with new leaves first and blossoms much later.

A kitchen noteRoll calamansi gently beneath your palm before cutting. It releases more juice and makes the seeds easier to catch.

What we pass along

Now the house belongs to another family, but the tree remains. Its canopy is wider, the old wall freshly painted. Once in a while, a photograph arrives: a bowl of fruit on the familiar sill, enough for everyone on both sides of the garden.

A recipe can travel on paper. A story can be repeated. A living thing asks for something else—the steady decision to return, notice what has changed, and care for what will outlast us.
