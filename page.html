About the journal

A record of things worth noticing.

Kahel is an independent journal about place, food, craft, and the quiet knowledge held in everyday routines.

“Kahel” is a familiar word for orange in the Philippines. It is also our reminder to pay attention—to color, season, memory, and the details that give ordinary days their shape.

The journal began as a shared notebook. Its earliest pages held market lists, overheard stories, recipes without measurements, and sketches made while waiting for a bus. Over time, those fragments became a way of looking more closely at the places around us.

Stories with time to breathe

We publish essays, conversations, photographs, and practical field notes. Some begin at a kitchen table; others follow a road, a river, or the history of an object made by hand. Each one starts with curiosity rather than certainty.

Look closely enough at an ordinary thing and it becomes a doorway into memory, place, and shared experience.

Our contributors write from different regions and disciplines, but they share a patient approach. They return to a subject, listen before interpreting, and leave room for what cannot be neatly resolved.

An open notebook

Kahel grows one issue at a time. Each collection follows a loose theme—harvest, shade, water, the walk home—while making room for unexpected connections. The archive remains open for readers who arrive later.

How we work

A few editorial principles.

These simple ideas guide what we notice, how we collaborate, and what eventually reaches the page.

01

Stay curious

Begin with a real question and follow where the details lead.

02

Listen carefully

Let people describe their own lives, work, and communities.

03

Share generously

Make useful knowledge clear, welcoming, and easy to return to.
